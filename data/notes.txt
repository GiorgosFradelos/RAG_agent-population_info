Demographics of Greece (2023‑2024 estimates):

- **Total population:** ~10.5 million people.
- **Population growth:** Slight decline of about -0.2% per year, driven by low birth rates and net emigration.
- **Age structure:**
  - 0‑14 years: ~14% of the population.
  - 15‑64 years: ~65% (working‑age population).
  - 65 years and over: ~21%, reflecting an aging population.
- **Median age:** Approximately 45.5 years (men 44.2, women 46.8).
- **Fertility rate:** Around 1.3 children per woman (well below the replacement level of 2.1).
- **Life expectancy:** Overall ~81.5 years (men ~78.5, women ~84.5).
- **Urbanization:** About 78% of Greeks live in urban areas; the largest metropolitan area is Athens (≈3.1 million in the metro region), followed by Thessaloniki (~1 million).
- **Ethnic groups:** Predominantly ethnic Greeks (>90%). Minorities include Albanians, Macedonians, Romani, and others.
- **Languages:** Greek (official). Minority languages include Albanian, Macedonian, Turkish, and Romani.
- **Religion:** Predominantly Greek Orthodox Christianity (~90% of the population); small Muslim, Catholic, and other religious communities.
- **Migration:** Net migration is negative, with more Greeks emigrating than immigrants arriving. Recent influxes of refugees and migrants have added to the foreign‑born population, now about 5‑6% of total residents.
- **Education:** High literacy rate (~97%). Tertiary education enrollment is around 45% of the 18‑24 age group.

These figures provide a snapshot of Greece’s demographic profile, highlighting an aging, slowly shrinking population with a high urban concentration in Athens and Thessaloniki.
